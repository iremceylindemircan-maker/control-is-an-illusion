# Rationale — Control Is an Illusion

## Idea

Control Is an Illusion is an interactive experience about the relationship between personal agency and the limits of a system. Participants begin with the ability to add, move and resize geometric shapes. However, their actions gradually remove available tools. The experience turns a familiar sense of digital control into a question: can users remain in control when their own decisions reduce their future possibilities?

## Premise A and B

The first premise, Agency, appears through the participant's ability to change the visual environment. Actions are not decorative clicks; they produce visible changes in the system. The second premise, Possibility, appears through the gradual locking of interaction tools. The system defines what can and cannot be done, and this available action space becomes smaller over time.

These premises depend on each other. Agency gives participants the ability to act, while Possibility determines the limits and consequences of those actions. The experience is built around this tension: participants actively shape the system, but cannot fully control the rules that shape their future choices.

## Theory

Wardrip-Fruin et al. (2009) describe agency as a relationship between the actions a participant wants to take and the actions supported by a system's computational model. This informed the decision to make the participant's actions meaningful while allowing the system to restrict later actions. However, the project is a small abstract experiment, not a complex narrative world; its model offers a deliberately limited form of agency.

Gaver (1991) explains affordances as possibilities for action that emerge through the relationship between people and their environment. His distinction between perceptible, hidden and false affordances informed the use of visible controls and locked buttons. The interface communicates that an action was once possible but is no longer available. However, Gaver's account primarily addresses how possibilities are perceived, while this project also uses restrictions as a conceptual statement.

## What I Learned

Testing the project myself helped me understand how interaction and restriction work together. I noticed that the experience becomes more meaningful as available tools gradually disappear. This made me realise that the interface does not only provide ways to interact; it also communicates the limits of control. Through this process, I learned how simple design decisions can express a broader concept.
